    <p>My Chinese name is 蓝剑锋. Lan (蓝), meaning "blue," is an uncommon surname. As far as I know, there are communities who share it in Guangxi. I am from Chongqing, though, and I am Han Chinese rather than a member of an ethnic minority. Growing up in a city famous for its hotpot also does not mean I can handle spicy food.</p>
    <p>Jianfeng (剑锋) means "the edge of a sword." In Chinese tradition, the hour of a person's birth is read through the Five Elements of wood, fire, earth, metal, and water, and a name can supply whichever element seems to be missing. My birth chart was found to be short on metal, so my uncle searched the dictionary for a character with the metal radical and landed on 锋. For years the name felt a little too sharp. Then I watched <em>The Lord of the Rings</em> and met Sting, the Elvish blade that glows blue whenever orcs are near. A blue blade, it turned out, was a pretty good thing to be named after.</p>
